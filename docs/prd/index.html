Overview

Police stations keep complaints, cases, criminals and evidence in scattered paper registers. Cross-referencing a suspect, following a case or producing a report is slow and error-prone.

CRMS centralises that data. A citizen files and tracks a complaint online. An officer or admin verifies it, registers a case, manages criminal and evidence records, and generates reports, all from one database.

Goals

  • Let a citizen report a crime without visiting the station, and see its progress.
  • Give officers one place for case, criminal and evidence data.
  • Enforce a single case lifecycle so no record skips a step.
  • Run the same schema and operations on MySQL, PostgreSQL and MongoDB.

Non-goals

  • Court proceedings and judgments.
  • Integration with external police databases.
  • Production-grade deployment; this is a lab prototype.
  • Citizen access to criminal, evidence or investigation data.

Users and roles

RoleWhat they doWhat they need
CitizenPublic user. Files and follows their own complaints.Report a crime without visiting the station; see progress.

Police adminStation-level owner of the system.Verify complaints, assign officers, manage users, produce reports.

Scope

In scope

  • Registration, login and role-based access.
  • Complaint filing, tracking and evidence upload.
  • Complaint verification and case registration.
  • Officer assignment and case status updates.
  • Criminal and evidence records.
  • Reports and user account management.

Out of scope

  • Court workflow after the chargesheet.
  • External police or government databases.
  • Payments, mobile apps and biometric data.

Quality requirements

IdQualityRequirement
NFR-01SecurityPasswords stored hashed (bcrypt or similar); role-based access checked on every request.
NFR-02PrivacyCriminal, evidence and investigation data is never visible to the citizen role.
NFR-03IntegrityPrimary and foreign keys enforced; no case without a complaint, no evidence without a case.
NFR-04AuditabilityEvery status change is logged with user, timestamp and old and new value.
NFR-05PerformanceRecord searches return in under 2 seconds on up to 100,000 records, using indexed keys and names.
NFR-06AvailabilityDaily database backup; recovery possible from the last backup.
NFR-07UsabilityA citizen can file a complaint in under 5 minutes without training.
NFR-08PortabilityThe same schema and operations run on MySQL, PostgreSQL and MongoDB.

Data model

The case is the hub. A complaint escalates to at most one case; the case links officers, a station, evidence, criminals and reports.

UseruserId, name, email, password, roleSpecialised into Citizen, Officer and Admin.
ComplaintcomplaintId, citizenId, description, dateFiled, statusCitizen 1 to many Complaint; escalates to 0 or 1 Case.
CasecaseId, complaintId, officerId, stationId, status, dateOpenedHub for Officer, Station, Evidence, Criminal and Report.
CriminalcriminalId, name, age, crimeHistoryCase many to many Criminal, through a junction table.
EvidenceevidenceId, caseId, type, description, dateCollectedWeak entity; composed in Case, deleted with it.

Environment

Hardware

  • Intel i3 or AMD Ryzen 3, or better.
  • 4 GB RAM minimum, 8 GB recommended.
  • 10 GB free disk space.
  • Network connection for multi-user access.

Software

  • Windows 10 or later, or Ubuntu 20.04 or later.
  • MySQL 8, PostgreSQL 14+, MongoDB 6+.
  • Front end in HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
  • Back end in Python or Node.js with database drivers.
  • MySQL Workbench, pgAdmin, MongoDB Compass.

Assumptions and risks

Assumptions

  • Each officer is posted at one police station at a time.
  • A complaint becomes at most one case; rejected complaints are kept, not deleted.
  • Only admins create officer and admin accounts; citizens self-register.

Risks

  • Keeping three database engines consistent raises the cost of every schema change.
  • Dialect gaps, such as auto-number keys and TRUNCATE, can break a script that ran on another engine.
  • Sensitive records need strict role checks from the first release, not as a later fix.
